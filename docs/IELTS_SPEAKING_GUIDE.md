# IELTS speaking practice

PairTalk is an independent practice tool. It does not administer an official IELTS exam or provide examiner-certified scores.

## Official format and criteria

The IELTS Speaking test lasts 11–14 minutes and has three parts: familiar-topic questions, a prepared individual long turn, and a broader discussion. For current format details, use [IELTS Speaking test format](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-academic-test/ielts-academic-format-speaking).

The four criteria are fluency/coherence, lexical resource, grammatical range/accuracy, and pronunciation. Use the official [Speaking assessment criteria](https://ielts.org/cdn/Guides/ielts-speaking-key-assessment-criteria.pdf) and [band descriptors](https://ielts.org/cdn/ielts-guides/ielts-speaking-band-descriptors.pdf) rather than treating a shortened app guide as the examiner rubric.

## A peer practice session

1. Agree who asks questions first and how much time you have.
2. Use Part 1 prompts to answer everyday questions with a reason or example.
3. Choose a cue card, allow preparation time, then give an uninterrupted response.
4. Discuss related ideas and follow-up questions for Part 3.
5. Swap roles and give specific feedback: where the answer was clear, where a word or sentence was difficult to follow, and one useful improvement.

You may use a timer or a permitted recording to review your own performance. A peer session need not match the exact official exam length, and the app does not guarantee an automated examiner-led sequence.

## Scores and feedback

PairTalk preferences are self-reported matching inputs. Some interfaces use whole-band choices; backend/account representations also support fractional values. This is a product input convention, not a claim about official examiner scoring.

Separate a partner's communication feedback or call-quality star rating from an IELTS band estimate. Peer feedback is subjective and should not be advertised as certification.
